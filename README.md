# LightsOut

## About

Lights Out is a free website that helps teens understand and track their sleep and stress, helping them better plan sleep schedules around academics and extracurriculars.

Each day, students complete a 30-second check-in: bedtime, wake-up time, and 1–5 ratings for sleep quality, stress, and mood. They can also tag what's happening, like a test, practice, or work shift. The Trends tab charts sleep against stress and tracks weekly averages, sleep debt, and bedtime consistency. The Insights tab finds patterns in the student's data, such as higher stress after nights under 7 hours or worse sleep after screen time before bed.

The Sleep Plan tab is built for busy schedules, specifically for middle and high school students. Students can import their class times through a screenshot of their schedule or type them in manually, then add practices, jobs, and homework. When a schedule doesn't allow enough sleep, the plan shows the most sleep possible and how much homework to move, and the AI Sleep Coach can suggest changes for those nights.

The AI Sleep Coach is connected to Claude through an API and optimized through prompt engineering to best help the student. It turns the student's patterns and schedule into three small changes for the week, with each recommendation tied to the student's data and backed by one of six peer-reviewed studies on mental health, sleep, and stress.

The app also includes crisis resources like the 988 Lifeline and a guided breathing exercise. Check-ins stay private on the student's device, no account is needed, and it runs on any browser, including school Chromebooks, so any student can use it.

## Research
Source	Key finding
Paruthi et al., 2016, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine	Teens need 8–10 hours. Too little is linked to depression, learning problems, and in teens, higher risk of self-harm.
Wheaton et al., 2018, CDC MMWR	72.7% of U.S. high schoolers sleep under 8 hours on school nights; 57.8% of middle schoolers in nine states get short sleep.
Weaver et al., 2018, JAMA Pediatrics	Students sleeping under 6 hours had about 3× higher odds of sadness and suicidal thoughts.
Verlenden et al., 2024, CDC MMWR	In 2023, 39.7% of high schoolers reported persistent sadness or hopelessness; 20.4% seriously considered suicide.
Baum et al., 2014, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry	Five nights of short sleep increased teens' anxiety and anger and weakened emotional control.
Lovato et al., 2017, Nature and Science of Sleep	Low mood predicted worse sleep later, so mood and sleep affect each other.
Hale & Guan, 2015, Sleep Medicine Reviews	About 90% of 67 studies linked screen time to shorter sleep and later bedtimes.
Drake et al., 2013, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine	Caffeine 6 hours before bed cut sleep by over an hour without people noticing.
